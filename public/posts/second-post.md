# 113 days

It has been 113 days since I quit my last position and took a chance to do something I've always wanted to do--pursue a mechanical engineering role. A lot of people tend to be afraid of taking chances on themselves, but I'm one of those people who will always advocate for pursuing your dreams; obviously within reason. Regardless, I'm not trying to be a Hollywood actor or anything outrageous. But even if it's something a little out there, I think it's probably worth exploring. I'm not gonna throw out clichés but we really do only live once. 

This extended time off has been a good time to reflect, not only about my career but life in general. I've also taken this time to improve my health since I've gained quite a bit of weight due to my sedentary lifestyle. Losing weight has been in my bucket list for a while, but now is the best time to get it in order. So far I've lost 20 lbs, which I'll say, it's not too shabby. I picked up running and I've been pretty consistent. It's not my favorite activity but I'm forcing myself to enjoy it since I know it's good for me. Running is convenient and it's known to be one of the most efficient ways to lose fat. I'm using this app called **Runna** to track my 5k running speed and I've shaved off about 10 minutes from my time in 8 weeks. Obviously, I'm nowhere near where I'd like to be so I'll continue to work on it and see how much time I can shave off by the end of the year.

Another thing that I've working on is preparing for the FE exam. And I have to say, I haven't had this much fun reviewing material in a long time. There's a reason I majored in mechanical engineering. The FE exam is really just another way to showcase my journey to become an expert in understanding how physical systems work. I've also been working through some CAD modeling exercises in SolidWorks from a book called *Design Workbook Using SOLIDWORKS 2026*, which contains a plethora of lab style exercises and problems from basic parts, assemblies, to FEA analysis. My idea is to use the book as a stepping stone for a personal projects portfolio. Populating a portfolio is going to take some and thought and time. I've always been interested in prosthetics, so perhaps some sort of medical device will be my first project.

And of course, I've been applying and conversing with potential employers. I have to say the job market is incredibly competitive. A single entry-level role will get thousands of applicants. It seems recruiters are a little skeptical about someone like me--an engineer who was worked mainly worked with software for the past 7 years looking to transition to mechanical engineering. Their skepticism is reasonable since they don't know the full story nor do they have the time to explore it. After all, I'm being judged on a single piece of paper. Then again, I've been targeting startups in the aerospace and robotics sectors which really have the pick of the litter. Nevertheless, this whole process doesn't dissuade me or demotivate me. On the other hand, it's something that I use to motivate myself to become the best mechanical engineer I can be. 

- *Marcelo Gonzales*
